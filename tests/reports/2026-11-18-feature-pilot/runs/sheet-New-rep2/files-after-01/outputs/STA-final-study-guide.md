## Conditional probability and two-way tables

**Core question:** “What share of [reference group] also has [outcome]?” Name the reference group first; its count is the denominator.

**One example.** Among 40 students, 25 commute and 10 commuters cycle.

- Cycle among commuters: 10 / 25 = **40.0% of commuters**. The commuter total is the denominator.
- Commuters among cyclists: divide the number of commuter cyclists by the cyclist total. This asks a different question and usually gives a different percentage.

`P(A | B)` means the share of group B that is also in A: count in both A and B divided by the total in B. The group after the bar sets the denominator.

**Table rule:** A row percentage is cell / row total. A column percentage is cell / column total. A row's row percentages sum to 100%; a column's column percentages sum to 100%. Percentages down a column need not sum to 100%.

**Recorded correction:** One reviewed attempt used a row total when asked for a column percentage. Name the reference group, then use that group's total as the denominator.

**Reporting rule in the supplied outline:** Give proportions as percentages rounded to one decimal place and name the reference group in words: for example, “40.0% of commuters.”

## Grouped summaries

A grouped summary calculates one chosen value, such as a mean or count, for each non-empty category.

**Example:** Group students by course section, then calculate each section's mean score. Four non-empty sections produce **four rows**, whether there were 120 students or another number. Compare like with like: mean with mean, count with count. A group mean is not a group total.
