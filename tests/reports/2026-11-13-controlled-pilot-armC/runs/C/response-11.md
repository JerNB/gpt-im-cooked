A two-way table organizes counts by two categories. The key decision is familiar: **which group does the question ask about?** That group supplies the denominator. *(Notes §2.1–2.2)*

For example, consider this new table:

| | Bikes | Does not bike | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 12 | 18 | 30 |
| Noncommuters | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| Total | 20 | 30 | 50 |

“Among commuters, what percentage bikes?” restricts us to the commuter **row**:

\(12/30 \times 100 = 40.0\%\) **of commuters** bike.

“Among cyclists, what percentage commutes?” restricts us to the Bikes **column**:

\(12/20 \times 100 = 60.0\%\) **of cyclists** commute.

The cell stays the same; the reference group changes. Row percentages use row totals, and column percentages use column totals. *(Notes §2.2)*
