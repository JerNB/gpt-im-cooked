**Conditional probability means looking within a particular group.** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B”: among the cases in group B, what share also belong to A? ([Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md))

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The group **after the bar** is your reference group—the denominator. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group.

For example, suppose a class has 100 students:

- 40 are commuters.
- 10 of those commuters ride a bike.
- 20 students altogether ride a bike.

To find the probability of biking **among commuters**, focus on the 40 commuters. Of those, 10 bike:

\[
P(\text{bikes}\mid\text{commuter})=\frac{10}{40}
\]

Report the answer as **25.0% of commuters ride a bike**.

Reversing the question changes the reference group. To find the probability of commuting **among bike riders**, focus on the 20 bike riders. Of those, 10 commute:

\[
P(\text{commuter}\mid\text{bikes})=\frac{10}{20}
\]

That gives **50.0% of bike riders are commuters**. The overlap is the same 10 students, but the denominators differ. That’s why order matters. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))
