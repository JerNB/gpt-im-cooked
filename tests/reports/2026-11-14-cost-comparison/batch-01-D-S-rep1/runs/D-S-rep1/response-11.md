A two-way table counts cases by two categorical variables. **Choose the reference group first; its total becomes your denominator.** A row percentage divides by the row total, and a column percentage divides by the column total. (*Your notes, §§2.1–2.2.*)

For example:

| | Cycles | Doesn’t cycle | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 6 | 14 | 20 |
| Residents | 9 | 11 | 20 |
| Total | 15 | 25 | 40 |

“Among commuters, what percentage cycle?” restricts us to the commuter **row**: \(6/20 \times 100 = 30.0\%\) **of commuters**.

“Among cyclists, what percentage commute?” restricts us to the cycling **column**: \(6/15 \times 100 = 40.0\%\) **of cyclists**.

The same cell supplies the numerator, but the question changes the denominator.
