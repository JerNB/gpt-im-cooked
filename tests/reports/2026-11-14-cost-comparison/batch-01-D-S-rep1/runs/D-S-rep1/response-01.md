**Conditional probability asks: within a particular group, what share has the characteristic we care about?**

Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A **among B**.” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is your **reference group**. [Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md)

For example, suppose a class has 100 students. Of these, 40 commute, and 10 of those commuters bike. This is a new example applying your notes’ rule.

To find the probability of biking **among commuters**:

- Restrict attention to the 40 commuters.
- Count the 10 bikers within that group.
- Divide 10 by 40.

Report the result as **25.0% of commuters bike**. The denominator is 40 because the question restricts us to commuters. [Notes §1.2](materials/notes.md)

**Order matters.** “What percentage of commuters bike?” and “What percentage of bikers commute?” use different reference groups. For the second question, we would need the total number of bikers, which this example hasn’t supplied. [Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md)
