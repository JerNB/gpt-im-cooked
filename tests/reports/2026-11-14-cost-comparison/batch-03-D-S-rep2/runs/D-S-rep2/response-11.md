A two-way table sorts cases by two categories at once. The key decision is familiar: **which group belongs in the denominator?** Row percentages use the row total; column percentages use the column total. ([Notes §2](materials/notes.md))

For example, consider this new table:

| | Cycles | Does not cycle | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 12 | 28 | 40 |
| Noncommuters | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| Total | 20 | 40 | 60 |

“Among commuters, what percentage cycle?” restricts us to the commuter **row**. We calculate \(12/40\): **30.0% of commuters cycle**.

“Among cyclists, what percentage commute?” restricts us to the cycling **column**. We calculate \(12/20\): **60.0% of cyclists are commuters**.

The overlap stays at 12; the reference group changes. Each complete row of row percentages totals 100%, and each complete column of column percentages totals 100%. ([Notes §2.2–2.3](materials/notes.md))
