**Conditional probability means “what share of this particular group has the property we care about?”**

Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A **among B**.” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is your **reference group**: the cases you restrict attention to. ([Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md))

For example, suppose 50 students include 20 commuters. Of those commuters, 8 ride a bike. Let \(A\) mean “rides a bike” and \(B\) mean “is a commuter.”

\[
P(A\mid B)
=\frac{\text{number in both }A\text{ and }B}{\text{number in }B}
=\frac{8}{20}
=40.0\%.
\]

Your answer should be: **“40.0% of commuters ride a bike.”** We divide by 20 because the question restricts us to commuters. The other students are outside that reference group.

**Reversing the condition changes the question.** Suppose 10 students in total ride a bike. Then \(P(B\mid A)\) asks what share of bike riders are commuters:

\[
P(B\mid A)=\frac{8}{10}=80.0\%.
\]

So **80.0% of bike riders are commuters**. The same 8 students appear in both calculations, but the denominators differ. That is why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) usually differ. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))

Remember: **first identify “among whom?” Then count the target cases within that group.** Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group. Your midterm also requires percentages to **one decimal place** and the reference group stated in words. ([Notes §1.4](materials/notes.md), [Outline, reporting rule](materials/outline.md))
