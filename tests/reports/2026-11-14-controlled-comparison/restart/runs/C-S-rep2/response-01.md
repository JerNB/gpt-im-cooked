**Conditional probability means finding a share within a particular group.** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.” Knowing \(B\) restricts which cases you consider. ([Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md))

The calculation is:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The denominator is **group B**, the reference group. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group.

For example, suppose a class has **40 commuters**, and **10 of those commuters cycle**. What is the probability that a student cycles, given that they commute?

“Given that they commute” tells us to consider only the 40 commuters. Among them, 10 cycle:

\[
P(\text{cycles}\mid\text{commutes})=\frac{10}{40}
\]

Your exam-ready answer is: **25.0% of commuters cycle.** Your [outline’s reporting rule](materials/outline.md) requires one decimal place and the reference group in words.

**Reversing the condition changes the denominator.** Suppose there are 25 cyclists altogether, including those 10 commuters. Then the share who commute *among cyclists* is **40.0% of cyclists**, calculated using 10 divided by 25. The overlap stays the same; the reference group changes. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))

Remember: **first identify the group after “given” or “among”; then count the target cases inside it.**
