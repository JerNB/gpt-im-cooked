A **two-way table** counts cases by two categories at once. Row and column percentages use the same reference-group idea you practiced: divide by the total for the group the question names. *(Notes §2.1–2.2.)*

Here’s an original example:

| Students | Bring lunch | Buy lunch | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 18 | 12 | 30 |
| Residents | 6 | 14 | 20 |
| Total | 24 | 26 | 50 |

**Among commuters, what percentage bring lunch?** Restrict attention to the commuter row. Of its 30 students, 18 bring lunch:

\(18 ÷ 30 × 100 = 60.0\%\) **of commuters**.

**Among students who bring lunch, what percentage are commuters?** Now restrict attention to the bring-lunch column. Of its 24 students, 18 are commuters:

\(18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75.0\%\) **of students who bring lunch**.

The numerator stayed 18. The denominator changed because the reference group changed. *(Notes §1.2–1.3; §2.2.)*
