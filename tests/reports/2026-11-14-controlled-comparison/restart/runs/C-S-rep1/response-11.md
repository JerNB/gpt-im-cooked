A two-way table organizes counts by two categories. Row and column percentages use the same reference-group idea you already practiced. ([Notes §2.1–2.2](materials/notes.md))

Here’s a new example using those rules:

| Students | Cycles | Does not cycle | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 12 | 18 | 30 |
| Residents | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| Total | 20 | 30 | 50 |

“Among commuters, what percentage cycles?” restricts us to the commuter **row**:

\(12/30 \times 100 = 40.0\%\) **of commuters**.

“Among cyclists, what percentage commutes?” restricts us to the cycles **column**:

\(12/20 \times 100 = 60.0\%\) **of cyclists**.

The cell stays the same; the reference group changes. Row percentages within one row add to 100%; column percentages within one column add to 100%. ([Notes §2.2–2.3](materials/notes.md))
