I’ll check your course materials and use their notation to explain conditional probability.

**Conditional probability means “what share within a particular group?”** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A **among B**.”

The group after the bar, \(B\), is your **reference group**. Restrict your attention to that group, then count how many also satisfy \(A\):

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

For example, your practice Q1 gives 40 students, including 25 commuters. Of those commuters, 10 cycle. What percentage **of commuters** cycle?

Your reference group is the 25 commuters, so calculate:

\[
\frac{10}{25}\times100\%=40.0\%
\]

**Answer: 40.0% of commuters cycle.** The class total of 40 isn’t the denominator because the question restricts us to commuters.

Order matters: “cycle **among commuters**” uses all commuters as the denominator; “commute **among cyclists**” uses all cyclists. Those can give different answers.

For your midterm, always report percentages to **one decimal place** and **name the reference group**. A useful habit: finish the sentence “Among ___” before doing any arithmetic.
