**Hypothesis testing asks whether observed data provide enough evidence against a starting claim.**

Suppose you want to investigate whether a coin is fair:

1. **State the claims.** The null hypothesis, \(H_0\), says the coin is fair. The alternative, \(H_A\), says it is biased.
2. **Collect data.** Flip the coin and count the heads.
3. **Calculate a p-value.** Assuming the coin is fair, how likely are results at least as extreme as the result you observed?
4. **Make a decision.** If the p-value is at or below a chosen significance level, reject \(H_0\). Otherwise, fail to reject it.

A small p-value means the data would be unusual under the null hypothesis. **It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.** Also, failing to reject a claim doesn’t prove it—it means the data don’t provide enough evidence against it.
