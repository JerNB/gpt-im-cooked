**Conditional probability means: within a particular group, what share has the property we’re interested in?**

Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A **among B**.” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is the **reference group**. [Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md)

For equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group:

\[
P(A\mid B)=
\frac{\text{number of cases in both A and B}}
{\text{number of cases in B}}
\]

Imagine 100 students. Of those, 40 are commuters, and 16 of those commuters drink tea.

If we ask, **“Among commuters, what percentage drink tea?”**, we focus on the 40 commuters. There are 16 tea drinkers inside that group, so the answer is:

**40.0% of commuters drink tea.**

The whole sample of 100 doesn’t enter this calculation because the question restricts us to commuters. That choice of denominator is the central idea. [Notes §1.2](materials/notes.md)

**Reversing the question changes the reference group.** Suppose there are 20 tea drinkers altogether. “Among tea drinkers, what percentage commute?” uses those 20 tea drinkers as its denominator. The same 16 students belong to both groups, giving:

**80.0% of tea drinkers commute.**

That’s why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) can differ: the overlap stays the same, but the reference group changes. [Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md)
