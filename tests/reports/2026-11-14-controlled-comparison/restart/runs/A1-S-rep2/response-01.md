I’ll check your course materials, then explain conditional probability using the notation and examples from your class.

**Conditional probability means “the probability of A among B.”** You restrict your attention to group B, then ask how many of those cases also belong to A.

Your notes write this as \(P(A\mid B)\). The vertical bar means “given”:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The key is choosing the **reference group—the denominator**.

For example, your first practice question says a class has 40 students, 25 commute, and 10 of those commuters cycle. What percentage **of commuters** cycle?

We focus on the 25 commuters. Of those, 10 cycle:

\[
\frac{10}{25}\times100\%=40.0\%
\]

So the answer is **40.0% of commuters cycle to campus.** The total class size doesn’t enter this calculation because the question restricts us to commuters.

Order matters: “What percentage of commuters cycle?” and “What percentage of cyclists commute?” use different reference groups and can have different answers.

1. Identify the group after “given” or “of.” That is your denominator.
2. Count how many in that group meet the requested condition.
3. Divide, convert to a percentage, and report **one decimal place plus the reference group**, as your outline requires.
