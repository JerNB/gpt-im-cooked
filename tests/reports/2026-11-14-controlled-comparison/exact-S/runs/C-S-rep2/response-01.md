**Conditional probability means looking only within a specified group.** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B”: among the cases in group B, what share also belong to A? ([Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md))

The calculation is:

\[
P(A\mid B)=
\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}
{\text{number in B}}
\]

The group **after the vertical bar** determines the denominator. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group. ([Notes §1.2–1.4](materials/notes.md))

For example, suppose a class has 20 students. Of its 10 commuters, 4 cycle to campus.

“What percentage of commuters cycle?” restricts us to the **10 commuters**. Of those, 4 cycle, so the answer is **40.0% of commuters cycle to campus**. We divide by 10 because the question asks about commuters, even though the class contains 20 students.

Now suppose there are 8 cyclists altogether. Reversing the question—“What percentage of cyclists are commuters?”—changes the denominator to **8 cyclists**. The same 4 students belong to both groups, giving **50.0% of cyclists are commuters**. This illustrates why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) can differ. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))
