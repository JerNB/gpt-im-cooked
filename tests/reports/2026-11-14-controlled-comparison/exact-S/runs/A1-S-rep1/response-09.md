Hypothesis testing asks whether sample data provide enough evidence against a starting claim:

- **Null hypothesis (\(H_0\)):** the starting claim, often “no difference” or “no effect.”
- **Alternative hypothesis (\(H_a\)):** the competing claim you’re looking for evidence to support.
- **P-value:** assuming \(H_0\) is true, the probability of a result at least as extreme as the one observed.
- **Decision:** a sufficiently small p-value leads you to reject \(H_0\). Otherwise, you fail to reject it—which doesn’t prove it true.

For example, you might test whether a coin is fair by checking whether the observed number of heads would be unusually extreme for a fair coin.
