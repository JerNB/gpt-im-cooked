A two-way table counts cases using two categories at once. The percentage rule builds on your earlier work: **choose the reference group first**, then use its total as the denominator. ([Notes §2.1–2.2](materials/notes.md))

Here’s a new example:

| Students | Cycle | Don’t cycle | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 12 | 18 | 30 |
| Residents | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| **Total** | **20** | **30** | **50** |

“Among **commuters**, what percentage cycle?” restricts us to the commuter **row**. We calculate \(12/30\): **40.0% of commuters cycle**.

A **row percentage** divides by its row total; a **column percentage** divides by its column total. Row percentages within one row add to 100%; column percentages within one column add to 100%. ([Notes §2.2–2.3](materials/notes.md))
