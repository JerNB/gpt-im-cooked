According to your [lecture notes, §1](materials/notes.md), **conditional probability means “the probability of A among B.”** You restrict your attention to group B, then ask what share also belongs to A.

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The key is the **reference group**: the group after the vertical bar supplies the denominator, rather than the whole sample. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group.

Order matters: “cycle among commuters” uses all commuters as the reference group; “commute among cyclists” uses all cyclists. These usually give different results.
