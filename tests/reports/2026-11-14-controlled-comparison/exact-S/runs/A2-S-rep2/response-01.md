According to your [lecture notes, §1](materials/notes.md), **conditional probability means looking within a particular group**.

\(P(A\mid B)\) reads “the probability of \(A\) among \(B\).” You restrict your attention to group \(B\), then find what share also belongs to \(A\):

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}.
\]

The key is the **denominator: use group \(B\), not the whole sample**. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group.

Order matters: “takes the bus among students living off campus” uses off-campus students as its reference group. Reversing it—“lives off campus among students taking the bus”—uses bus riders instead, so the probabilities can differ.
