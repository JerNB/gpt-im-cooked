**Conditional probability means “what share within a specified group?”** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is your **reference group**. [Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md)

For equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number of cases in both A and B}}{\text{number of cases in B}}
\]

Imagine 100 students. Of these, 40 commute, and 16 of those commuters cycle.

To find the probability that a student cycles **given that they commute**, focus on the 40 commuters. Of those, 16 cycle. Your answer is:

**40.0% of commuters cycle.**

The denominator is 40 because the question restricts us to commuters. Dividing by all 100 students would answer a different question: what share of *all students* both commute and cycle?

**Reversing the condition changes the reference group.** Suppose there are 20 cyclists altogether. Asking what share of cyclists commute now uses the 20 cyclists as the denominator. The answer is **80.0% of cyclists commute**. The same 16 students appear in both calculations, but we compare them with different groups. That is why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) usually differ.
