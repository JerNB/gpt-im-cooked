**Conditional probability asks: within a particular group, what percentage also meets another condition?**

Your notes write this as \(P(A\mid B)\), read “the probability of \(A\) among \(B\).” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is the **reference group**.

To calculate the percentage:

\[
\frac{\text{number in both }A\text{ and }B}{\text{number in }B}\times100\%
\]

For example, your practice set describes 40 students, including 25 commuters. Of those commuters, 10 cycle to campus.

For “What percentage **of commuters** cycle?”, restrict your attention to the 25 commuters:

\[
P(\text{cycle}\mid\text{commuter})
=\frac{10}{25}\times100\%
\]

**40.0% of commuters cycle to campus.**

The total of 40 students isn’t the denominator because the question asks about commuters specifically.

Order matters: “What percentage of **cyclists** commute?” would use all cyclists as the reference group. We aren’t given that total, so we can’t answer that reversed question.

For your midterm, use this routine:

1. Identify the reference group—the group after “among,” “given,” or “of.”
2. Count how many in that group meet the other condition.
3. Divide by the reference group’s total.
4. Report a percentage to **one decimal place**, naming the reference group.
