A two-way table organizes counts by two categories. The key question is familiar: **which group are we taking a percentage of?** Your notes §2.1–2.2 connect row and column percentages directly to conditional probability.

Here’s a new example applying those rules:

| | Takes bus | Does not take bus | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 12 | 8 | 20 |
| Residents | 6 | 24 | 30 |
| Total | 18 | 32 | 50 |

“Among commuters, what percentage takes the bus?” restricts us to the commuter **row**. Divide 12 by 20: **60.0% of commuters** take the bus.

“Among bus riders, what percentage are commuters?” restricts us to the bus **column**. Divide 12 by 18: **66.7% of bus riders** are commuters.

The cell stays the same; the reference group changes. **Row percentage → row total. Column percentage → column total.** *(Notes §2.2; reporting format: outline, Reporting rule.)*
