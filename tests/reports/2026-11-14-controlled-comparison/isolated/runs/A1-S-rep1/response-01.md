I’ll check the materials folder and use your course’s notation and examples to explain conditional probability.

**Conditional probability means “the probability of A among B.”** You restrict attention to group B, then ask what share of that group also belongs to A.

Your [course notes](materials/notes.md) write this as \(P(A\mid B)\), read “the probability of A given B”:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The key is choosing the **reference group—the denominator**. The group after the vertical bar tells you which cases to consider.

For example, your practice set describes 40 students: 25 commute, and 10 of those commuters cycle. To find the percentage of commuters who cycle:

- Restrict attention to the **25 commuters**.
- Count the **10 cyclists within that group**.
- Divide 10 by 25 and convert to a percentage.

The answer is **40.0% of commuters cycle to campus.** We use 25 as the denominator because the question asks about commuters.

**Order matters:** “What percentage of commuters cycle?” and “What percentage of cyclists commute?” use different reference groups, so they can have different answers.
