Hypothesis testing uses sample data to assess a claim about a population:

- **Null hypothesis (\(H_0\))**: the starting claim, often “no difference” or “no effect.”
- **Alternative hypothesis (\(H_1\))**: the competing claim you’re investigating.
- **P-value**: assuming the null hypothesis is true, the probability of obtaining a result at least as extreme as the observed result.
- **Decision**: compare the p-value with a chosen significance level, \(\alpha\). If it is at or below \(\alpha\), reject \(H_0\); otherwise, fail to reject it.

For example, you might test whether a coin is fair by flipping it repeatedly and checking how surprising the result would be under the fair-coin assumption.

Two key cautions: **a p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true**, and failing to reject the null does not prove it.
