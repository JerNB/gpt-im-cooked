**Conditional probability means finding a share within a specified group.** Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A **among B**.” The vertical bar means “given”: we know the case belongs to B, and ask whether it also belongs to A. ([Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md))

Using counts,

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}.
\]

The denominator is **the group after the bar**. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group.

For example, suppose a class has 100 students:

- 40 are commuters.
- 10 of those commuters bike to campus.
- Altogether, 20 students bike to campus.

What is the probability that a student bikes, **given that they commute**? Restrict attention to the 40 commuters. Of those, 10 bike:

\[
P(\text{bikes}\mid\text{commutes})=\frac{10}{40}.
\]

Report this as: **25.0% of commuters bike to campus.**

Reversing the question changes the denominator. Among the 20 students who bike, 10 commute:

\[
P(\text{commutes}\mid\text{bikes})=\frac{10}{20}.
\]

So **50.0% of students who bike are commuters**. The overlap is the same 10 people, but the reference groups differ. That is why reversing a conditional probability usually changes its value. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))
