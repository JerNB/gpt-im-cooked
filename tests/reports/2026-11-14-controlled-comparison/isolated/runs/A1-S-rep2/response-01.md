**Conditional probability is the probability of something within a specified group.** Your notes describe \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.”

The key is to **restrict your attention to group B**:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The group after the bar is your **reference group**, so it determines the denominator.

For example, your practice set describes 40 students, including 25 commuters. Of those commuters, 10 cycle. To find the percentage of commuters who cycle, focus on the 25 commuters:

\[
\frac{10}{25}\times100\%=40.0\%
\]

Report this as: **“40.0% of commuters cycle.”** The total of 40 students isn’t the denominator because the question restricts us to commuters.

Order matters: “What percentage **of commuters** cycle?” and “What percentage **of cyclists** commute?” use different reference groups and can have different answers.

For the midterm, use this routine:

1. Identify the reference group.
2. Count how many in that group also meet the condition.
3. Divide by the reference-group total.
4. Report a percentage to **one decimal place**, naming the reference group.
