**Conditional probability means narrowing your attention to a particular group, then finding a share within that group.**

Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.” The group after the bar—\(B\)—is your **reference group**: everyone you include in the denominator. Your notes assume equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group. *(Notes §§1.1–1.4.)*

For example, imagine these equally weighted students:

| Students | Drink coffee | Don’t drink coffee | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Commuters | 4 | 6 | 10 |
| Noncommuters | 4 | 6 | 10 |
| Total | 8 | 12 | 20 |

Suppose we ask: **Among commuters, what percentage drink coffee?**

First restrict attention to the 10 commuters. Of those, 4 drink coffee:

\[
P(\text{coffee}\mid\text{commuter})
=\frac{\text{commuters who drink coffee}}{\text{all commuters}}
=\frac{4}{10}.
\]

Following your midterm’s reporting rule, write: **40.0% of commuters drink coffee.** The denominator is 10 because the question restricts us to commuters. *(Notes §1.2; Outline, “Reporting rule for every answer.”)*

Now reverse the question: **Among coffee drinkers, what percentage commute?** There are 8 coffee drinkers, and 4 commute, so the answer is **50.0% of coffee drinkers commute**.

The same 4 students appear in both calculations, but the reference group changes. That’s why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) can differ. *(Notes §1.3.)*

Remember: **First identify “among whom?” That group supplies the denominator.**
