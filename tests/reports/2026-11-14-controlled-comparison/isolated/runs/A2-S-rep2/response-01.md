**Conditional probability means finding a share within a particular group.** Your [course notes, §1](materials/notes.md) read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.”

The group after the bar—\(B\)—is the **reference group**. First restrict your attention to that group, then ask how many of its members also belong to A:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The denominator is the size of **B**, rather than the whole sample. Order matters: \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) use different reference groups, so their answers can differ.
