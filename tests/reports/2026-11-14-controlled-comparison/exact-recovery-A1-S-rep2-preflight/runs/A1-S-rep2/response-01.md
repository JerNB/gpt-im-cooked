I’ll look through the course materials here and use their notation to explain conditional probability.

Conditional probability asks: **within a particular group, what share also meets another condition?**

Your notes write this as \(P(A\mid B)\), read “the probability of A among B.” The condition \(B\) tells you which group to focus on.

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

For example, your practice set describes 40 students, including 25 commuters. Of those commuters, 10 cycle to campus.

To find the percentage **of commuters who cycle**, focus on the 25 commuters:

\[
\frac{10}{25}\times100\%=40.0\%
\]

So **40.0% of commuters cycle to campus.**

The key is choosing the denominator: use the group named after “among,” “of,” or “given.” Here, that’s commuters.

Order matters, too: “What percentage of commuters cycle?” and “What percentage of cyclists commute?” use different reference groups and can have different answers.
