**Conditional probability asks: within a particular group, what share has the property we care about?**

Your notes read \(P(A\mid B)\) as **“the probability of A among B.”** The group after the bar—\(B\)—is the **reference group**, so it supplies the denominator:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number of cases in both A and B}}{\text{number of cases in B}}
\]

This assumes equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group. ([Notes §1.1–1.4](materials/notes.md))

For example, suppose a class has 100 students:

- 30 are commuters.
- 12 of those commuters bike to campus.
- 20 students in the whole class bike.

What is the probability that a student bikes, **given that they are a commuter**?

“Given that they are a commuter” restricts our attention to the **30 commuters**. Among those students, 12 bike. Dividing 12 by 30 and converting to a percentage gives **40.0% of commuters bike to campus**.

Now reverse the question: what share of **students who bike** are commuters? The reference group becomes the 20 students who bike. Dividing 12 by 20 gives **60.0% of students who bike are commuters**. The overlap stayed the same; the denominator changed. That’s why \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\) usually differ. ([Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md))
