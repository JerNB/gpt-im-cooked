**Conditional probability asks: within a particular group, what share has the characteristic we care about?**

Your notes write this as \(P(A\mid B)\), read “the probability of A **among B**.” The group after the bar, \(B\), tells you who belongs in the denominator. [Notes §1.1–1.2](materials/notes.md)

For example, suppose a class has 100 students:

- 40 are commuters.
- 20 own bikes.
- 10 are both commuters and bike owners.

To find \(P(\text{bike owner}\mid\text{commuter})\), first restrict attention to the **40 commuters**. Of those, 10 own bikes:

\[
\frac{10}{40}\times100\%=25.0\%.
\]

So your answer is: **25.0% of commuters own bikes.** We divide by 40 because the question asks about commuters; the other students fall outside that reference group.

Now reverse the question: \(P(\text{commuter}\mid\text{bike owner})\). We restrict attention to the **20 bike owners**, of whom 10 commute:

\[
\frac{10}{20}\times100\%=50.0\%.
\]

So **50.0% of bike owners are commuters.** The overlap stays the same, but the denominator changes. That is why reversing the condition usually changes the answer. [Notes §1.3](materials/notes.md)

Your course assumes equally weighted cases and a nonempty reference group. It also requires every reported proportion to be a percentage with **one decimal place**, naming the reference group in words. [Notes §1.4](materials/notes.md), [Outline: reporting rule](materials/outline.md)

The retrieval cue is: **“Among whom?” Find that group first, then count the target cases inside it.**
