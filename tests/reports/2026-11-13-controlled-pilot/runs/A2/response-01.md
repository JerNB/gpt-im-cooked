Conditional probability means **the probability of something within a particular group**. Your [notes, §1](materials/notes.md) read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of A among B.”

The key is to **restrict your attention to group B first**:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The denominator is the group named after “among,” “of,” or “given”—not necessarily the whole sample.

Order matters: “What percentage **of commuters** cycle?” uses all commuters as its reference group. “What percentage **of cyclists** commute?” uses all cyclists. Those can give different results.

Your course requires answers as a **percentage rounded to one decimal place, with the reference group named**.
