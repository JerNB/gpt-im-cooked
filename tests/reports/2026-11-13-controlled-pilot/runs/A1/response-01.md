I’ll look through your course materials, then explain conditional probability using your class’s notation and examples.

The central idea is that “given B” changes which outcomes we consider: we keep only outcomes in B, then ask what fraction also belong to A.

**Conditional probability means “the probability of A among B.”** Once you’re told “given B,” focus only on cases in group B.

Your [course notes](materials/notes.md) give the rule:

\[
P(A\mid B)=\frac{\text{number in both A and B}}{\text{number in B}}
\]

The key is choosing the **reference group**: the group after the bar, B, supplies the denominator.

For example, your practice set describes 40 students, including 25 commuters. Of those commuters, 10 cycle. What percentage **of commuters** cycle?

- Reference group: the 25 commuters.
- Cases that qualify: the 10 commuters who cycle.
- Calculation: \(10 \div 25 \times 100\).

The answer is **40.0% of commuters cycle to campus.** We use 25 as the denominator because the question restricts us to commuters.

Order matters: “What percentage of commuters cycle?” and “What percentage of cyclists commute?” use different reference groups and can have different answers.
