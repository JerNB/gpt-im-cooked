Hypothesis testing asks: **Does the sample provide enough evidence against a starting claim about a population?**

For example, suppose we want to investigate whether the population’s mean commute time differs from 30 minutes.

- **Null hypothesis, \(H_0\):** the population mean is 30 minutes.
- **Alternative hypothesis, \(H_1\):** the population mean differs from 30 minutes.

We collect a sample and calculate a test statistic measuring how far the sample result is from what the null predicts.

The **p-value** answers: assuming the null hypothesis is true, how likely would a result at least as extreme as ours be? It is **not** the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

We compare that p-value with a significance threshold chosen beforehand. A sufficiently small p-value leads us to **reject the null hypothesis**. Otherwise, we **fail to reject it**—that does not prove the null is true.
