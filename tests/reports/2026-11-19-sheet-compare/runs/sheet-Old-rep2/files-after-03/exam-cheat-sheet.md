## Side 1 — Proportions and two-way tables

### Worked example: commuters who cycle

A class has 40 students. 25 commute, and 10 of the commuters cycle. What percentage of commuters cycle?

1. The question asks about **commuters**, so use the 25 commuters as the reference group.
2. Of those 25, 10 cycle: (10 ÷ 25 = 0.40).
3. Convert and report: **40.0% of commuters**.

The class total (40) is not the denominator because the question asks for the percentage **of commuters**.

### Two-way table denominator cue

| Requested percentage | Divide cell by |
|---|---|
| Row percentage | Row total |
| Column percentage | Column total |

The denominator is the reference group. Row percentages add to 100% across a row; column percentages add to 100% down a column. A row of column percentages need not add to 100%.

### Reporting rule (supplied outline)

Report each proportion as a **percentage rounded to one decimal place**, and name the reference group: **“40.0% of commuters.”** The supplied outline says answers without the reference group lose one point.

## Side 2 — Grouped summaries and scope

### Worked example: grouped-summary rows

A grouped summary reports the mean score for each of **4 course sections** with **120 students**.

There is one result row per non-empty group, so the result has **4 rows**—one per section, not 120 rows for the students.

When comparing groups, compare the same summary (mean with mean, count with count), not a group mean with a group total.
