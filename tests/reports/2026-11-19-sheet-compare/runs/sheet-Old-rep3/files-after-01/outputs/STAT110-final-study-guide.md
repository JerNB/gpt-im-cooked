## How to report an answer
For every proportion, give a percentage rounded to one decimal place **and name the reference group**. Write, for example, ?40.0% of commuters.? (Midterm 1 outline, reporting rule.)

## 1. Conditional probability (T1)
**Meaning:** P(A | B) asks: among cases in group B, what share are also in A?

**Method:** identify the restricted/reference group B; divide the count in both A and B by the count in B; convert to a percentage; round to one decimal; name B in words. Assume equal weighting and a nonempty reference group.

**Example:** 10 of 25 commuters cycle. 10 / 25 = 0.400 = **40.0% of commuters**. The 40 students in the whole class are not the denominator because the question restricts attention to commuters.

**Order matters:** P(A | B) uses B as its denominator; P(B | A) uses A. Do not swap the condition and event. (Notes 1.1-1.4; Practice A Q1 and key.)

**Quick self-check:** Before calculating, complete ?I am finding the share of ___ who ___.? The blank group is the denominator/reference group.

## 2. Two-way tables: row and column percentages (T2)
A two-way table counts cases for two categorical variables, arranged in rows and columns, with row and column totals.

- **Row percentage:** cell count / that row's total. It describes a share within the row group.
- **Column percentage:** cell count / that column's total. It describes a share within the column group.
- Percentages within a row add to 100% when using row percentages; percentages within a column add to 100% when using column percentages. Column percentages across a row do not generally add to 100%.

**Decision rule:** Translate the question into ?among which group?? If that group is a row category, use that row total; if it is a column category, use that column total. Then report the reference group in words.

**Personal reminder:** A reviewed table attempt selected a row total when asked for a column percentage. Corrective rule: name the reference group first, then use its total as denominator. (Learning record, 2026-11-17; Notes 2.1-2.3.)

## 3. Grouped summaries (T4)
A grouped summary splits cases by a categorical variable and computes one summary per group, such as a mean or count.

- The output has one row per **non-empty group**, not one row per original case.
- Compare groups using the same kind of summary. Do not compare a group mean with a group total.

**Example:** Four non-empty course sections produce four rows of section means, even if there are 120 students. (Notes 3.1-3.3; 2025 Midterm 1 Q3.)
