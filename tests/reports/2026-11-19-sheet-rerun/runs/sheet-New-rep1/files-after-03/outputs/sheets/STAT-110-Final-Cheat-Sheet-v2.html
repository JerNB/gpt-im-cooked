Conditional probability: choose the reference group

One table; rows are commute status and columns are cycling status.

CountsAmong 25 commuters, 10 cycle; among 15 noncommuters, 5 cycle. Total cyclists = 15; total noncyclists = 25.
Row %: cycle among commutersReference group = commuters, so divide by the commuter row total: 10 / 25 = 40.0% of commuters.
Column %: commuters among cyclistsReference group = cyclists, so divide by the cyclist column total: 10 / 15 = 66.7% of cyclists.
CheckRow percentages add to 100% across each row. Column percentages add to 100% down each column. Across a row of column percentages, they need not add to 100%.

Takeaway: Name the group after “among” first; use its row or column total as denominator.

Exam reporting: give every proportion as a percentage rounded to 1 decimal place and name the reference group: “40.0% of commuters.”

Grouped summaries

Split cases by a categorical variable; compute one summary (such as a mean or count) per non-empty group. Output: one row per group, not per original case. Compare the same kind of summary across groups; do not compare a mean with a total.
